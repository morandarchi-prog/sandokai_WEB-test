Iaido

The way of the sword

Iaidō is a Japanese martial art centred on drawing the sword and cutting in a single movement. Its practice brings together technical precision, body control and awareness.

01 /

Movement and mindset

Studying iaidō engages both body and attention. Gaze, posture, footwork and sword handling work together to give each movement its coherence.

At Sandokai, particular attention is paid to body dynamics and the timing of movements. Through repetition and observation, students refine their coordination and learn to perform techniques with precision and a non-aggressive attitude.

02 /

Practising kata

Training consists primarily of kata: set sequences performed individually against imagined opponents. Each form explores a specific situation and is practised from a standing or kneeling position.

Kata generally follow four stages: drawing the sword and making an initial cut, following with a main cut, performing chiburi — the symbolic gesture of cleaning the blade — and completing nōtō, returning the sword to its scabbard.

Beyond the sequence itself, students explore the intent behind each movement, spatial awareness and sustained alertness.

03 /

The Musō Shinden Ryū school

At Sandokai, we practise Musō Shinden Ryū iaidō, a school whose modern form was developed by Nakayama Hakudō in the twentieth century.

Its curriculum includes three groups of kata — Shoden, Chūden and Okuden. Their progressive study deepens students’ understanding of the techniques and the principles behind them.

Each student progresses at their own pace, with guidance from the teachers. As movements become more refined, practice offers an opportunity to cultivate patience, concentration and self-awareness.
